If your teenager disappears into headphones, don't panic. That may be their safe space.

Be curious, even if the music your kid loves doesn't land with you. There is something in the sound, or the lyrics, or the scene that has very deep meaning for who they are and who they're becoming. Ask them what they're listening to and why they're into it. A tiny question can become a conversation that might not happen any other way.

Music isn't just entertainment. For a young person it is emotional regulation, cultural belonging, identity, and resilience. It's how many of them regulate stress, explore who they are, feel understood, and connect with other people. Simply engaging with a kid about the music they love can tell you a lot about where they're at.

## Three things to try

**Ask for the song, not the feeling.** "What are you listening to right now?" is an easier door than "how are you doing?" If they play it for you, listen to the whole thing before you say anything.

**Trade one back.** Share the song that found you at the right time, and the story behind it. It models that it's normal to have a hard time and to have something that helped.

**Notice the shift, not the genre.** A change in what they listen to, how loud, how much, or how alone can be worth a gentle question. The music itself, however dark it sounds to you, usually isn't the problem; more often it's how they're coping.

Music doesn't replace therapy or other support, and it isn't a substitute for talking to a professional if you're worried. But it can be a bridge. If you or your teenager needs help now, call or text 988, or find local, affordable resources in our [Get Help directory](/resources/).
